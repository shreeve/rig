# `List.push` inline: measured, not taken (2026-11-06)

The json panel's item 3 (panel/zig.md E1): `List.push` read its length again after storing the element, since the store may alias the List to LLVM, and LLVM left the push out of line at some of std.json's sites. Rig agreed to try it as its own change, landing only on Trig's bar and never slower on std.json. The change (`list-push/runtime.diff`): `List.push` inline, reading `len` once; `String.push` reading its length once. Inlined too, `String.push`, `pushInt` and `pushKeep` cost gsoc-2018 3.4% more instructions, so they stayed calls.

## Measured (the Mac), against main 14a0e754b and an A/A copy
- **Instructions:** std.json 1.3-3.3% fewer on all six files (bench/json/guard.py); bench/hash and bench/text level.
- **std.json time** (fastest of 20 interleaved runs, `list-push/json.txt`): twitter 2.0% faster, citm_catalog 2.2%, twitterescaped 0.9%; canada 0.6% slower, github_events 0.5% slower, and **gsoc-2018 3.0% slower**, with 1.8% fewer instructions. The A/A arm within 0.6%.
- **Trig** at one checker and at four, 20 interleaved rounds (`list-push/t1.csv`, `t4.csv`): instructions level to 0.18% more, paired cycles 0.6% fewer to 2.1% more (mitt's 2.1% at one checker against its A/A arm's 1.3% fewer), so no gain. Trig's binary 12.9 KB larger (0.04%).

It clears neither bar: Trig gains nothing, and std.json is slower on gsoc-2018. Not taken; the branch is the tag `archive/list-push`.
