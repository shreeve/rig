# std.text against memchr and Go, and the checks its search proves (2026-11-06)

The Mac, Apple M5, lib.dom.d.ts (2,349,483 bytes), `bench/text/time.rig`'s 72 cases, fastest of N runs in turns. Rig main 22e7cbb00.

## The standing

`bench-text --rivals`, 20 rounds: std.text built with `--release` and `--release=fast`, the memchr crate 2.8.3 (`memmem::find`, and a `memmem::Finder` built once per case), and Go's `bytes.Index`. All four agree on where each needle is. Against std.text's `--release=fast` build:

- **The whole text, needles of 3 to 40 bytes:** 17-30% faster than memchr, and 1.3 to 17 times faster than Go.
- **Texts of 8 to 64 bytes, needles of 3 to 40 bytes:** std.text leads, or is within 6% of the Finder.
- **12 and 40 bytes in 1 to 4 KiB:** 13-60% behind the Finder, whose work on the needle is done once. Against `memmem::find`, which takes its needle at each call as `text.find` does, std.text is behind only on the 12-byte miss in 4 KiB, by 13%.
- **One byte in 16 B to 4 KiB, misses and late hits:** about twice as slow as memchr, as Zig's `findScalar` searches there.
- **`--release`, Zig's safe mode:** 12-44% slower than `--release=fast`, from the bounds and overflow checks in the search loops.

## Taken: the checks the search proves (`rig.proven_checks`)

Each search function out of line, and the inline helpers they use, holds `@setRuntimeSafety(rig.proven_checks)`, with a comment naming why its indexes are in range. This is the runtime's form for checks that its own invariants prove (src/runtime.zig, `proven_checks`): kept in a debug build, left out of an optimized one.

The inline `search`, which `find` and `count` put where they are called, keeps Zig's checks. With proven checks there, its caller's code changed, and a 1-byte search of 64 bytes was 6% slower in Zig's safe mode.

**Measured:**
- **`--release=fast`:** the program's code is identical to main's (one md5 of `__text`), since that mode has no safety checks.
- **`--release`, against main, 20 rounds with an A/A arm:**
  - Every case of 3 to 40 bytes is 12-44% faster: 22% at 16 bytes, 31-34% at 1 KiB, and 37-44% over the whole text.
  - The 1-byte miss over the whole text is 26% faster.
  - The 1-byte cases below 4 KiB are level with main within the A/A arm's spread. The timing loop, with its inline search, is the same instructions as main's.
- **The guard** (instructions per search, `--release`): 31-43% fewer on every row but `line`, which is 0.4% fewer.

## Measured and not taken: a 1-byte search of 16 and 64 bytes a step

Ceiling arms through `RIG_STD`, `--release=fast`, 10-20 rounds each:

- **The first 32 bytes tested 16 at a time, then 64 bytes a step, all inline:**
  - Every 1-byte case is 17-86% faster, which closes memchr's lead: 16 B miss 0.5 ns against memchr's 1.3, 4 KiB miss 34 against 34.
  - But cases of 3 to 40 bytes moved. The needle's length is known only at run time in `time.rig`, so both paths are inlined into its loop, and a 64-byte late hit of 12 bytes was 4% slower in every arm.
  - The 4 KiB 40-byte miss was 10% slower. That was code placement: `far`'s code was identical and only 4 bytes over, and main with `far` alone moved to a 64-byte boundary was 12% slower too.
  - Pinning `searchMany` to 64 bytes fixed that row, but moved short rows.
- **The 64-byte loop out of line:** short rows level, but a hit at byte 90 of the whole text 5-12% slower, by the call.
- **A compact inline loop of 16-byte steps over the first 128 bytes:** 1-byte hits at 1-4 KiB 16-81% slower.
- **Dropped earlier:**
  - 64-byte steps from 0 or 16: early hits slower.
  - The 64-lane `maskBits`: slower.
  - Four inline blocks first: everything slower.
  - The whole 1-byte search out of line: early hits slower by the call.

So the 1-byte search's gain is real, but it trades about 0.1 ns on short multi-byte cases and on early hits for it. That is left for a change of its own.
