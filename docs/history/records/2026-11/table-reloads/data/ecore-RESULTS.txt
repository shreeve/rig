## Who issues the extra loads (all_loads samples with data address, T, share of all loads)
Loads that read the Checker object itself: webpack 18.3%, typeorm 16.8%, Compiler 17.7% (23-25% of loads in checker
functions). Table heads (store.*.slots) 5.4-5.8%, program 2.0-2.5%, ids 1.0-1.7%, binder_syms 0.9-1.0%, syms,
flow_memo. In absolute loads these match Trig's excess over tsrs: typeorm 0.25G vs excess 0.24G, Compiler 0.075G vs
0.070G, webpack 0.47G vs 0.23G. Spread thin: no function holds more than 0.35% of all loads in them.

## What blocks retirement (ld_head PEBS, D)
Table-head loads are 0.1% of the blocking loads (they hit L1 and finish early); the slot loads after them block.
Blocking loads spread over AST lists (~25%: u32 words 9%, NodeRec 8.5%, NodeLoc 3.7%), store Tables (~10%), maps;
no chain above ~2%. tsrs: its AST node header 23%. Load-latency (>=16) samples: store Tables ~3-4% of L1-miss
latency; memcpy 17%.

## Reading
The memory-bound excess on the E-core is load-buffer occupancy (ld_buf blocks 4.7-9.7x tsrs, +4.7-6.3% of cycles),
from about 10-20% more load uops per instruction, and those extra loads are reloads of Checker fields (Table heads,
program, ids, binder_syms) after calls and stores. Not miss latency (L2-miss/LLC stalls are at or below tsrs), and
not one chain of Tables.
