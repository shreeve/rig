# Where the split cost Trig, and the rule that keeps it out (2026-11-05)

Profiled on pup in a timing window (03:23-03:28), the same arms as [trig-pup-2026-11-05.md](trig-pup-2026-11-05.md):
- `perf record -F 4000 -e cycles:u` and `perf stat` of Trig's ts-pattern, on core 3 at one checker;
- `even-tasks` from bench/tasks, on core 6 at one worker.

One release build compiled on other cores, so the profile shares and the instruction counts are the results.

## ts-pattern

Split against main:
- **instructions:** 8,425M to 8,687M, 3.1% more;
- **branches:** 1,590M to 1,644M, 3.4% more;
- **cycles:** 3,668M to 3,738M, 1.9% more.

The largest change in `perf diff` is `checker.Checker.mapper_map`, from 4.8% to 6.6% of the samples. Its hot arms are linear searches that return at their first match:

```rig
.Array
  sources = (?@).mapper_sources(m)
  for i in 0..sources.len()
    return (?@).type_at((?@).mapper_targets(m), i) if t == (?@).type_at(sources, i)
  return t
```

A mapper has one to a few sources, so the loop runs a few turns. Its block's start costs more than the checks it saves: the turns left, the block's end, and the outer loop's test. The other changes are small and scattered, and several symbols are only in one build's profile: LLVM inlined differently around the bigger loop bodies.

## even-tasks

On x86, in this quieter run: 1,155M to 840M instructions (27% fewer), and 903M to 927M cycles (2.7% more). The earlier row of 56% more cycles was the release build sharing the machine. There, a check on each turn is cheap enough (a `dec` fused with its jump) that the blocks gain little time.

## On the Mac: the check at the top of the body

Moving `spin`'s check to the top of its body, kept on every turn, takes 7% fewer cycles than today's (827M against 891M at one worker). It still leaves one extra add on the chain each turn waits on: 7 steps, not Rust's 6. Only the block form reaches Rust's loop (705M).

## The rule

The foreman approved it on 2026-11-05: split only a range that always runs to its end. Its body holds no loop, and no `break`, `return`, failure passed on (`!`, or `??` with a jump), or labeled `continue` (emit's `leavesEarly`, outside closures).
- A search, which may stop at its first turns, keeps today's code exactly. `mapper_map` and the other searches of its kind emit as they did on main.
- A split loop never leaves a block early, so no jump gives turns back, and the refund is gone. The probe [sched-probe.rig](sched-probe.rig) still prints the same under seeds 1 to 20 as main's rig, with two of its loops split.
