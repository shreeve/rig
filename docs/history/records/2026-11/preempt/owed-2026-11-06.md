# A task that lives in system calls: the yield check a signal owes (2026-11-06)

## What the #987 review found
On main, a `RIG_PREEMPT=signal` release build with 4 workers ran 8 tasks looping on `file.read` (with sleeps of 0 or 1 ms) and one task sleeping 20 ms. The sleeper's timer did not fire within 1 s in 12 of 150 runs.

## Reproduced and measured (Mac, then x86-64 Linux on burst, correctness runs)
- **The starving builds:**
  - Mac: 51 of 100 runs starved, against 0 of 100 for the plain build.
  - Mac, the sleeper's wake: 0.3 s to 4.8 s; median 200 ms with `file.read` alone.
  - Linux x86-64: 1.8 s, then 17.6 s; runs whose `main` also sleeps hit a 10 s timeout.
- **Variants that sleep or spin without `file.read`:** never starved, woke in 20-46 ms.
- **`RIG_SCHED_STATS=1`:**
  - Each worker ran 2 to 12 fibers in all.
  - The monitor sent each one 155 to 158,000 signals, and all but one to three per worker found the fiber "outside the program's code".

## The cause
`file.read` of a regular file reads in the task: on a local disk the system call costs less than a trip to the pool. So a loop of reads never parks, and spends almost all its time in `stat`, `open`, `read` and `close`, and in the runtime's copying.

The handler stops a fiber only where its program counter is in the program's own code (`inCode`), so nearly every signal did nothing. Four such tasks held the four workers. The timers due on them waited for each worker's 61-turn `service`, which never came: the 20 ms sleeper, the 1 ms sleeps of the other readers, and `main`'s own.

A plain build has yield checks in the loop, which fire due timers and hand over the worker. Linux shares the cause: a signal that arrives during a system call is delivered as it returns into libc, also outside the program's code.

## The fix
- **The handler records the debt.** When a signal finds the fiber outside the program's code, or under a lock, the handler sets its worker's `owed` flag.
- **The next waiting point pays it.** `rt.checkStop`, the start of every call that reaches the file system or a socket, calls `sched.atWaitingPoint`. When the flag is set and no lock is held, that clears it and runs the yield check the signal asked for (`sched.tick`): it fires due timers, then hands over the worker if work waits.
- **A build without `RIG_PREEMPT=signal` is unchanged:** the call is under `if (rt.preempt)`.

**After the fix, Mac:**
- The sleeper wakes in 21 ms every time.
- 0 of 100 runs starve, and the `file.read`-only variant wakes at a median of 20 ms, max 20.
- Signals per worker fall to 3-8.

`test/cli/preempt.sh` gains `reads.rig`: the sleeper must wake within 2 s in each of five runs. main's rig fails it in 5 of 10 runs; the fix passes 10 of 10.

## What stays open
A loop that spends its time outside the program's code with no waiting point is still stopped only where a signal finds its own code. Such a loop calls no file or socket function: it sorts by a runtime comparator, or allocates and frees in a loop. The yield check stays owed until that loop's next waiting point.
