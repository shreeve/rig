Read borrows now have one representation, decided by a single structural rule, so parameters, results, and locals always agree, and a borrow of large data is never silently copied.

## The rule

- **Copied:** a read borrow of a scalar or a view: a number, `Bool`, `String`, a slice, a function or borrowed callable, a plain enum, an error, or an optional of one of these. Copying it costs no more than a pointer, and nothing can change what it sees.
- **Lent by address (`*const T`):** a read borrow of anything else, meaning structs, arrays, payload enums, and generic instances. As before, anything that owns resources or holds a `Cell` is also lent by address.

The emitter (`readBorrowIsPtr`) applies the rule to Rig types, and `rig.ReadBorrow` applies it to Zig types for generic code. The rule is structural, not size-based, so the two cannot disagree.

## What it fixes (all present on main)

- A read-borrow local rebound to a function's `?T` result failed in Zig. The local held a pointer while the function returned a copy. This was recorded as a known test in PR #16 and is now a passing behavior test.
- `fun first(x: ?[1000000]Int)` copied 8 MB on every call. It now takes `*const [1000000]i64`, called as `first(&a)`. That copy broke the "no hidden costs" rule, and it made the 16 MiB frame check undercount stack use, since the check counts a borrow as a pointer.

## Emitted Zig

| Rig | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `fun first(x: ?[1000000]Int)` | `x: [1000000]i64` (a copy) | `x: *const [1000000]i64` |
| `fun sum(p: ?Pair)` | `p: Pair` (a copy) | `p: *const Pair` |
| `fun inc(n: ?Int)` | `n: i64` | `n: i64` (unchanged) |
